title: Having a life outside work
pubDate: 2026-10-25
draft: false
---

I like knowing what people care about outside work.

Someone who is usually quiet in meetings starts talking about a film they loved and suddenly has a lot to say. Someone else has spent the weekend trying to get a recipe right. You get to know a part of them that wouldn't come up in a conversation about the next release.

I think that makes working together more interesting.

You can care deeply about your work and still have thoughts that have nothing to do with it. I would want the people I work with to have room for both. And I would expect their lives outside work to look quite different from mine.

That doesn't mean everyone needs an impressive hobby. Spending time with family counts. So does being very invested in a television show. There shouldn't be another standard to live up to once you close your laptop.

What worries me is a culture where work takes up so much space that there is little room left for anything else. Even when the hours are reasonable, every conversation can end up being about the company. You start feeling like spending your attention elsewhere means you care less.

I think a team loses something when that becomes normal. If everyone spends most of their time thinking about the same problems, the conversations get narrower. There are fewer chances to hear something you wouldn't have thought about yourself.

Different lives give people different things to say. Sometimes that changes how you think about a problem at work. Often, it just makes a conversation more enjoyable. Hearing someone talk about something they care about makes me want to know more, even if it's something I wouldn't have picked up myself.

It's one of the things I like about working with different people. There is always a chance to get interested in something that wasn't on my mind before.
