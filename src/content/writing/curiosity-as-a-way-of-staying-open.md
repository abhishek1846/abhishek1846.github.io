---
title: Curiosity as a way of staying open
pubDate: 2026-10-24
draft: false
---

I started getting curious about security when we were working on our own startup. I wanted to understand more about the space, even though I wasn't planning a career in it at the time.

That interest stayed with me. Later, I was at Kloudfuse, which was a good company to be in. Security was still something I wanted to explore, and that curiosity eventually led me to Oleria.

Looking back, I can connect those things quite easily. While it was happening, I didn't know where the interest would go. I was just curious.

I think about that when I catch myself dismissing something as a distraction because it isn't immediately relevant to what I'm doing.

At work, there is always more you could understand. Someone mentions a problem their team is dealing with and you want to know why it happens. You hear a customer explain how they do something and find yourself asking more questions than you need to finish your task.

I like working with people who do that. They are interested enough to ask, even when the answer has no obvious use for them.

In my case, an interest did lead to a change in direction. Plenty of other things I've been curious about haven't led anywhere in particular. I don't think I needed to get something out of those for them to be worth exploring.

Curiosity can keep you open to work you might otherwise never consider. It doesn't automatically create an opportunity or tell you what to do with one. Mostly, I think being interested is a good quality to hold on to, at work and outside it.

I'd like to keep following things that interest me without needing to explain where they'll lead.
