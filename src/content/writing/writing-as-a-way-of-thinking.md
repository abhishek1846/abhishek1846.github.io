---
title: Writing as a way of thinking
pubDate: 2026-10-24
draft: false
---

Some of the most clarity I've had at work has come from sitting down and writing things out.

An idea would seem clear enough in my head. I could talk about it in a meeting and feel like I understood what we needed to do. But when I started writing it down, I would find gaps I hadn't noticed before.

Sometimes I couldn't explain why one part followed from another. Sometimes a decision depended on an assumption I hadn't really thought through. It was much easier to skip over those things in a conversation.

Writing made me spend time with them.

It also helped me find new ideas. While trying to explain a problem, I would notice another way to approach it. Or I would realise that a small change made the original idea much stronger. I hadn't sat down expecting to come up with anything new. I was trying to write what I already thought I knew.

That's probably what I find most useful about it. I can go back to a sentence and ask whether I actually believe it. I can read a paragraph and see that it doesn't explain what I thought it did.

The first version is usually messy. Some parts are unclear, and there are questions I still need to think through. If I try to make it sound good too early, I end up spending time on sentences I might need to delete anyway.

Often, nobody else needs to read that version. By the time I share something, a lot of the useful work has already happened in the rough notes.

I still talk things through with people. But when I'm going in circles on something, writing it down is usually what helps me see where I'm stuck.
